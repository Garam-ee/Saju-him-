korean birth chart red flags, sorted

Most “red flag” lists are personality traits wearing a warning label. In saju, five patterns genuinely repeat — and three of the scariest-sounding ones say almost nothing about how he treats you.

a red flag is a pattern, not a prophecy

A chart is eight letters and the relationships between them. It describes the machinery: how someone decides, argues, spends and leaves. Machinery repeats. What he does with it is still his to do.

So the useful question is never is this chart bad? It is how often does this show up, and does he know it’s there? A man who knows he goes quiet after a decision is a different man from one who swears he never has.

the five that actually repeat

Read them as weather, not verdicts. Each one ends with the thing actually worth watching.

metal · he decides without announcing it. 金 metal is the clean cut. A metal-heavy chart finishes a decision at blade speed and then behaves as if everyone was in the room for it. The quiet afterwards isn’t punishment — the decision was done before your last message. Watch: whether he ever shows you one while it’s still soft.

water · he recedes, he doesn’t leave. 水 moves with temperature, not with feeling. A busy week, a cold room, a mood he can’t name: he thins out, then reappears at 2am with a paragraph. That is not a goodbye. Water doesn’t do goodbyes. Watch: whether he names the temperature afterwards, or leaves you to guess it. The same disappearing act three times is a pattern, not a mood.

fire · everything for two weeks, then nothing. 火 cannot burn in one place. Fourteen days of calls, plans and a shared calendar, then a flat week where he’s fine and you feel dropped. It isn’t cruelty; fire goes where the oxygen is. Watch: whether he comes back and explains the flat week, or acts like it never happened.

earth · he never decides, and it costs you a year. 土 is the most expensive chart to date, because it never says no. Sometime. Let’s see. Soon. Six months of almost. Earth isn’t lying — it moves at geological speed. Watch: whether the answer gets more specific with time, or just warmer.

a crowd of peers · 비겁 bigyeop, the rival star. Peer characters share his element: his friends, his crowd, his competition. The classical logic is blunt — in a man’s chart the spouse star is the wealth star, and the peers are the ones who take it. In 2026 that reads as: he keeps a field wide, and he hates losing more than he enjoys winning. Watch: how you get introduced. Not “does he have female friends” — everyone does. Whether you’re a person in his life or an option inside it.

the three that aren’t red flags

These have become household words in Korea and get translated into English as warnings. They aren’t.

도화살 dohwa-sal, peach blossom. Charm, visibility, kki — flair, the thing that makes a room turn. Old texts filed it under scandal and applied it more harshly to women; modern Korean readers use it as stage presence. It describes how visible he is, not how faithful.

역마살 yeokma-sal, post horse. Movement — travel, relocating, working abroad, restlessness. The whisper network translates it as “he’ll leave you.” It means he doesn’t sit still well.

화개살 hwagae-sal, canopy. Solitude, art, reflection. In month one it reads as cold and unavailable. Usually it means he needs one evening a week that belongs to nobody, including you.

and the clash everyone misreads

If your day branches clash, someone will tell you it’s a doomed pairing. Your day branch is the spouse palace, and a clash between two of them is real — friction, volume, the same fight in different words. It is one of five comparisons that build the gunghap number, and the number summarises the working rather than delivering a verdict.

quick answers

Is a peach blossom star a cheating flag? No. Visibility, not fidelity.

Does a day-branch clash mean we should break up? No. It names where friction lands, not whether the two of you can carry it.

Can I read his chart without his birth time? Yes. And if you’d rather not ask, the calculator can send him a link that makes him fill in his own.

what a chart will not tell you

Whether he’s faithful. Whether he comes back. Whether you should stay. A chart describes a structure and where two structures rub. Anyone who turns that into a promise about next March is selling something worse than a $19 PDF.

The flags are only here so you stop filing a pattern as a personality problem — his, or yours.
